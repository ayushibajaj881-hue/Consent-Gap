# ConsentGap — Privacy Policy vs. Browser Activity

ConsentGap is a Chrome extension that compares two things:

1. **What a website says in its privacy policy**
2. **What the browser actually sends to third parties**

Instead of simply blocking trackers, ConsentGap tries to explain whether observable browser activity may be inconsistent with a specific privacy-policy claim.

> **Important:** A tracker request does **not automatically prove** that a website violated its policy or the law. ConsentGap reports a **potential consent mismatch** based on observable browser evidence and the policy claims it can identify.

---

## The Problem

Users often accept cookies and privacy permissions without an easy way to understand what happens afterward.

A privacy policy may make a statement such as:

> “We do not share your personal information with third parties.”

But users generally do not have a simple way to compare that statement with observable browser activity.

Existing privacy tools often focus on **blocking** trackers. ConsentGap focuses on **explaining the relationship between privacy claims and browser activity**.

---

## The Solution

ConsentGap is a Chrome extension that:

- Finds the website's privacy-policy link.
- Extracts recognizable privacy-policy claims using deterministic rules.
- Observes relevant browser network requests.
- Identifies third-party requests and known tracker domains.
- Detects recognizable data categories such as **Email**, **User / device ID**, and other supported fields.
- Compares the policy claims with the observed activity.
- Produces an explainable **Consent Gap Score**.
- Shows the evidence directly in the extension popup.

### Core flow

```text
Website
   |
   +--> Privacy Policy
   |       |
   |       v
   |   Policy Claims
   |
   +--> Browser Network Activity
           |
           +--> Third-party Requests
           +--> Known Trackers
           +--> Recognizable Data Types
                     |
                     v
              Compare Evidence
                     |
                     v
             Consent Gap Score
                     |
                     v
             User Explanation
```

To make the prototype testable, we created a controlled website called **DemoShop**.

This gives us known ground truth: we control both the privacy-policy text and the requests generated by the website.

The DemoShop contains three scenarios.

The policy contains a no-third-party-sharing statement and the test generates no third-party data request.

## Scenario 2 — Tracking only

**Observed result: 2 — Low Consent Gap**

## Scenario 3 — Controlled mismatch

The policy contains a **“No third-party sharing”** statement.

The controlled website then generates a third-party request containing recognizable data fields.

ConsentGap detects:

- 1 third-party request
- 1 data request
- Email
- User / device ID
- Search / product activity
- The relevant privacy-policy claim
- A potential consent mismatch

**Observed result: 82 — High Consent Gap**

![DemoShop — controlled mismatch: ConsentGap detects a policy/activity conflict and shows a score of 82.](screenshots/Screenshot%20%2852%29%281%29.png)

# How the Extension Works

### 1. Privacy Policy Detection

`content.js` looks at the webpage and identifies a privacy-policy link.

### 2. Policy Analysis

`policy-phrases.js` contains explainable rules for common policy statements.

For example:

```text
"We do not share your personal information with third parties"
                |
                v
       no_third_party_sharing
```

### 3. Network Observation

`background.js` observes relevant requests and records signals such as:

- destination
- GET/POST method
- first-party vs. third-party
- known tracker status
- recognizable data category

The prototype records the **data type/category**, rather than intentionally storing the actual personal-data value.

### 4. Comparison

`popup.js` combines:

- observed browser activity
- recognizable data types
- extracted policy claims

It then generates the score and explanation.

---

# Score Calculation

The prototype uses a simple weighted scoring model that is easy to explain:

| Signal | Maximum contribution |
|---|---:|
| Policy mismatch | 50 points |
| Recognizable data exposure | Up to 30 points |
| Third-party requests | Up to 20 points |
| **Maximum** | **100** |

The score is an **indicator of observed signals**, not an accuracy percentage, legal-compliance score, or proof of a policy violation.

A higher score means the prototype found more signals that may warrant investigation.

---

# Data Detection

ConsentGap does not need to retain the actual personal-information value to display the relevant category.

For example:

```text
email=someone@example.com
```

is represented as:

```text
Email
```

Similarly:

```text
user_id=12345
```

is represented as:

```text
User / device ID
```

This is intended to reduce the privacy risk created by the extension itself.

# Installation

1. Unzip the project.
2. Open Chrome.
3. Go to:

```text
chrome://extensions
```

4. Enable **Developer mode**.
5. Click **Load unpacked**.
6. Select the `consentgap-extension` folder.
7. Pin ConsentGap.
8. Open a normal website.
9. Reload the website after installing/updating the extension.
10. Click the ConsentGap extension.

After changing extension code, click Chrome's **Reload** button for ConsentGap on `chrome://extensions`, then reload the website.

# Presentation Explanation

### 30-second explanation

> “ConsentGap is a Chrome extension that checks the difference between a website's privacy promises and its observable browser activity. We monitor relevant requests, identify third-party and known tracker activity, and look for recognizable data types such as email and user IDs. We then compare these observations with simple claims extracted from the privacy policy. Instead of calling every tracker a violation, we flag a potential mismatch when the observable evidence conflicts with a specific policy claim.”

### If asked: “How do you detect trackers?”

> “We maintain a starter list of known tracker domains and also compare the page's origin with the destination origin to identify third-party requests.”

### “How do you know what data is being sent?”

> “For requests where the browser exposes request information, we search for recognizable field names and patterns such as email and user_id. We store the category, not the actual value.”

### “How do you read the privacy policy?”

> “We find the privacy-policy link on the page, fetch its text through the extension, and use explainable keyword rules to identify claims such as no third-party sharing.”

### “Why not use AI?”

> “For our first prototype, we chose deterministic rules because they are fast, transparent and easy to explain. AI or NLP can be added later to understand more complicated policy language.”

### “Does a tracker mean the company is doing something wrong?”

> “No. A tracker alone does not prove a policy violation. Our extension distinguishes tracking activity from a potential policy mismatch.”

### “What is innovative?”

> “Traditional privacy tools mostly focus on blocking trackers. ConsentGap focuses on the relationship between what a website's privacy policy claims and what we can observe in browser activity.”

# Limitations

This is a prototype. It cannot:

- See every possible kind of browser data.
- Understand every privacy-policy sentence.
- Prove the legal meaning of a request.
- Decrypt arbitrary encrypted traffic.
- Reliably detect data that is encoded or obfuscated beyond the current rules.
- Guarantee that every third-party request is a tracker.
- Guarantee complete visibility into all data exchanged by a website.

These limitations are important because browser-level observation is not the same as complete server-side visibility.
